Read the switches like this. SCENE = the full build: the window frosts the desktop (DWM — free, live) and the two panes frost app content (MultiEffect — +13 pp CPU each). WINDOW = DWM only; the panes go opaque — and STRUCTURAL becomes AMBIENT. FLAT = RDP / high-contrast: no glass, no shadow, no specular. Zero information is lost at any rung. Underlay probe slides a plot under the spine: the spine refuses to frost a hot-path island and paints its opaque card pre-blend instead — watch it pop, and note that the pop is a visible state change in the material (weakness 4 in the .md).

Here STRUCTURAL breaks its own promise. _TriggerDialog, _ROIDialog and device_panel are separate top-level windows. An in-scene MultiEffect cannot reach the parent window's content — measured. So the surface most obviously "above your work" is the one surface that cannot frost it. STRUCTURAL's fix is to convert both dialogs into in-window overlays: real code in scope_panel.py and camera_panel.py. That is scope, not polish.
